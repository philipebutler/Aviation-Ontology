Semantic Maturity Prompt Templates and Glossary

Progressive Semantic Development from Glossary to Operational Ontology

Purpose

This document combines:

* the semantic maturity prompt templates,
* and the semantic glossary

into a single operational reference for organizations beginning semantic architecture development.

The document is intended to support:

* semantic discovery workshops
* ontology development
* MBSE semantic alignment
* digital engineering transformation
* enterprise terminology governance
* AI-enabled engineering initiatives
* digital thread architecture development
* and semantic governance activities.

The framework is intentionally designed for organizations that may be:

* early in MBSE adoption,
* heavily document-centric,
* operating with legacy semantics,
* or beginning semantic modernization.

The goal is not to create a perfect ontology immediately.

The goal is to progressively:

* externalize meaning,
* expose ambiguity,
* formalize relationships,
* stabilize semantics,
* and create computable enterprise understanding.

Recommended Workflow

For best results:

1. Start with a small number of concepts.
2. Avoid trying to model the entire enterprise immediately.
3. Prefer operationally important concepts.
4. Use real engineering artifacts and terminology.
5. Capture disagreements rather than forcing premature alignment.
6. Preserve historical and local meanings.
7. Treat semantic development as iterative.
8. Expect semantic discovery to reveal organizational inconsistencies.

Good initial concepts include:

* Requirement
* Function
* System
* Part
* Verification
* Change
* Interface
* Configuration Item
* Program
* Certification Artifact

LEVEL 0

Glossary Development

Purpose of This Layer

The glossary layer attempts to:

* externalize terminology
* stabilize language
* capture informal meaning
* expose ambiguity
* identify conflicting usage

At this stage the goal is NOT:

* semantic precision
* formal ontology
* enterprise normalization

The goal is:

“What words are people using and what do they think those words mean?”

⸻

Level 0 Input

The ideal starting input is:

Term: Requirement
Definition: A formal engineering obligation.

or:

List of terms with informal definitions.

⸻

Level 0 Interview Template

We are developing an enterprise semantic architecture.
For the following term:
TERM:
[Insert Term]
CURRENT DEFINITION:
[Insert Existing Definition]
Please answer the following questions.
1. How is this term commonly used in practice?
2. What does this term mean operationally?
3. What kinds of artifacts or activities is this term associated with?
4. Who typically uses this term?
5. Are there multiple meanings of this term?
6. Does the meaning change between departments or tools?
7. Are there legacy meanings that differ from current meanings?
8. Are there abbreviations or alternate names?
9. What misunderstandings commonly occur around this term?
10. What other terms are commonly confused with this one?
11. What examples best illustrate the term?
12. What examples are commonly mistaken for this term but should not be?
Do not attempt to normalize terminology yet.
Capture ambiguity and variation explicitly.

Glossary of Terms

Abstraction

Abstraction is the process of representing something at a higher conceptual level while hiding unnecessary implementation detail.

Example:

* “Flight Control System” is more abstract than specific actuators and wiring.
* “Function” is more abstract than physical hardware.

⸻

AI Participation

AI participation refers to the ability for AI systems to:

* interpret enterprise semantics,
* reason about engineering relationships,
* understand lifecycle context,
* assist decision-making,
* and participate in workflows.

⸻

Artifact

An artifact is any managed engineering output or information object.

Examples:

* requirement documents
* CAD models
* test reports
* specifications
* certification evidence

⸻

Attribute

An attribute is a property or characteristic of an entity.

Examples:

* part number
* requirement priority
* owner
* lifecycle state

Attributes describe:

properties of an object

Relationships describe:

participation in a system

⸻

Canonical Definition

A canonical definition is the enterprise-authoritative meaning of a concept.

Canonical definitions provide the preferred enterprise semantic interpretation.

⸻

Computable Semantics

Computable semantics means that meaning is represented in a form that systems can:

* interpret
* reason about
* validate
* automate

⸻

Conceptual Ontology

A conceptual ontology formally describes:

* what concepts fundamentally mean
* how concepts differ
* what abstraction layer concepts belong to
* what semantic boundaries apply

⸻

Constraint

A constraint is a rule limiting what is considered semantically valid.

Example:

* Every Requirement must have an owner.

⸻

Controlled Vocabulary

A controlled vocabulary is a governed set of approved enterprise terminology.

It defines:

* preferred terms
* approved abbreviations
* accepted synonyms
* deprecated terminology

⸻

Dependency

A dependency exists when one concept, object, or activity relies on another.

Dependencies are foundational to systems reasoning.

⸻

Digital Thread

Digital thread refers to connected lifecycle relationships linking:

* requirements
* design
* verification
* manufacturing
* certification
* operations

Digital thread is fundamentally relationship-centric.

⸻

Entity

An entity is a distinct concept or object represented within the enterprise semantic architecture.

Examples:

* Requirement
* Function
* Part
* System
* VerificationArtifact

⸻

Governance

Governance refers to the processes and controls used to maintain semantic consistency and authority.

⸻

Inference

Inference is the ability to derive new conclusions from existing semantic relationships or rules.

⸻

Knowledge Graph

A knowledge graph is a graph-based representation of interconnected semantic entities and relationships.

⸻

Lifecycle State

A lifecycle state describes the maturity or operational status of an entity.

Examples:

* Draft
* Approved
* Released
* Verified
* Obsolete

⸻

Logical Ontology

A logical ontology extends conceptual ontology by introducing:

* rules
* constraints
* inference
* computable semantics

⸻

MBSE

Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) is a systems engineering approach emphasizing:

* formal models
* connected semantics
* explicit relationships
* model-centric engineering information

rather than primarily document-centric engineering.

⸻

Ontology

An ontology is a formal representation of:

* concepts
* meaning
* relationships
* constraints
* semantic rules

within a domain.

⸻

Relationship Model

A relationship model defines:

* how entities interact
* how dependencies propagate
* how meaning connects across the enterprise

This is the layer where:

Disconnected data becomes connected meaning.

⸻

Semantic Architecture

Semantic architecture is the enterprise structure used to represent:

* meaning
* concepts
* relationships
* constraints
* lifecycle semantics
* governance

⸻

Semantic Drift

Semantic drift occurs when meanings gradually diverge over time.

⸻

Semantic Graph

A semantic graph is a network of entities connected through explicit semantic relationships.

⸻

Semantic Maturity

Semantic maturity describes how explicitly and operationally enterprise meaning has been represented.

⸻

Semantic Model

A semantic model is a structured representation of meaning.

A semantic model may include:

* entities
* attributes
* relationships
* constraints
* lifecycle semantics

⸻

Semantic Relationship

A semantic relationship describes how concepts meaningfully interact.

Examples:

* satisfies
* verifies
* realizes
* allocated_to
* affects

⸻

Systems Reasoning

Systems reasoning refers to reasoning about:

* dependencies
* interactions
* lifecycle propagation
* constraints
* emergent system behavior

⸻

Taxonomy

A taxonomy is a hierarchical classification structure used to organize concepts.

Taxonomy answers:

“How are concepts grouped and organized?”

⸻

Traceability

Traceability is the ability to follow semantic relationships across lifecycle artifacts.

⸻

Tribal Knowledge

Tribal knowledge refers to organizational knowledge that exists informally in people’s experience rather than explicitly in enterprise systems.

One major goal of semantic architecture is:

externalizing implicit knowledge into explicit computable semantics.
